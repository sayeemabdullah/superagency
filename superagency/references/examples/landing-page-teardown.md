# Example — Landing Page Teardown

> **Illustrative only.** Every company, number, and quote below is invented for shape. Never reuse these figures, and never present them as evidence.

---

## Ledgerly /demo — ordered by expected impact

**1. The headline names the category, not the benefit — blocking**
Currently: "Modern financial close software."
That describes what it is, not what changes. A visitor who doesn't already
know they want close software has no reason to keep reading.
Try: "Close the books in a day, not a week." Offer 2-3 variants and test.

**2. Message mismatch with the ad — blocking**
The paid search ad promises "free 14-day trial." The page's only CTA is
"Book a demo." That gap is the single most likely cause of the 1.2% conversion
rate. Either honour the trial or change the ad — do not do both at once, or you
won't know which fixed it.

**3. Eleven form fields — high**
Phone, company size, role, and "how did you hear about us" are all optional to
the sale. Every field costs conversions. Cut to email plus company; ask the rest
on the call.

**4. Competing CTAs — high**
The hero says "Book a demo," the mid-page says "Start free," and the footer says
"Talk to sales." Three different asks. Pick one.

**5. Proof is below the fold and generic — medium**
Six logos with no context, 1,400px down. Move one specific result next to the
CTA. A named number beats six anonymous logos.

**6. The objection isn't handled — medium**
Nothing on the page addresses migration effort, which is the reason finance
teams don't switch. One line near the CTA would do it.

**7. Mobile CTA is three screens down — medium**
Most traffic is mobile. The button should be reachable without hunting.

---

**Where the funnel is actually leaking**
Ask for the step-by-step numbers before acting on any of the above: traffic →
page view → form start → form complete → qualified. A 1.2% overall rate could
be a traffic-quality problem, not a page problem, and the fixes are different.
Do not guess between them.

**If they're testing:** change one thing at a time. Headline first — it moves
numbers more than anything else on this list.
