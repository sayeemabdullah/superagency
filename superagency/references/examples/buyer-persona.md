# Example — Buyer Persona

> **Illustrative only.** Every company, role, number, and quote below is invented for shape. Never reuse these, and never present them as evidence.

---

## Persona: "Priya", VP Finance — *hypothesis, not yet validated*

*Basis: 6 sales calls and 3 churn interviews. Not yet checked against product analytics or a win/loss review — treat as a hypothesis to test.*

### Role
VP Finance or Controller at a 20-200 person company. Owns the close, reports to the CFO or CEO, two to five people on the team.

### What she's measured on
Days to close, audit findings, and whether the board deck lands on time. A slipped close is visible to the whole executive team — this is the pain that carries budget.

### Jobs to be done
- **Functional:** produce an accurate close, faster, without hiring.
- **Emotional:** stop dreading the first week of every month.
- **Social:** be the finance lead who never makes the board wait.

### Pains and current workarounds
Reconciliation is manual across three systems. She keeps a personal spreadsheet of "things that always break." Overtime in close week is assumed, not questioned.

### Where she learns
Two finance-operations newsletters, a Slack community for controllers, and peers she has worked with before. Does not attend large conferences.

### Buying role
Champion and economic buyer for deals under ~$25k. Above that the CFO signs and IT runs a security review.

### Objections she raises
- "We just did an implementation project — I can't put the team through another."
- "How is this different from a feature in our accounting suite?"
- "What happens to the audit trail?"

### What would validate or break this persona
- Analytics: do trial accounts with a "Controller" title activate faster? (supports)
- Win/loss: is "days to close" the stated reason for purchase, or is it price? (tests the core assumption)
- If most deals actually close through the CFO, the buying-role section is wrong.
