# Pricing & Packaging

Covers: price changes, tier design, packaging, discounting, price objections.

Often pairs with: `positioning.md` (what the price signals), `competitive.md` (what the market charges), `budget.md` (what the model has to return).

## Before advising
Ask three things: what the product costs to deliver, what the current price and plan structure are, and what's prompting the question — losing deals, leaving money on the table, a new segment. Pricing advice without the first two is a guess; label it one.

## Packaging
- Tiers map to *segments and their willingness to pay*, not to feature count. Three tiers is the common shape; past four, buyers stall.
- Each tier needs one reason to move up — the value metric that grows with the customer (seats, usage, revenue processed).
- Name what you meter on before you set numbers. Changing the value metric later is a re-platform, not a price change.

## Changing a price
- Model the change against the existing base separately from new customers. A number that works for new logos can churn the base.
- Grandfather or migrate deliberately. A silent increase on existing accounts is the fastest route to a backlash.
- State the assumption that most affects the outcome — take rate, churn sensitivity — and what happens if it's wrong.

## Discounting
Routine discounts train buyers to wait, and mean the list price is wrong. Time-box and reason-code every discount so the pattern is visible.

## Price objections
"Too expensive" is usually a value-communication problem or a wrong-segment problem, not a price problem. Diagnose which before moving the number.

## Output
Packaging proposals and pricing-change plans → file, with an assumptions block at the top. Quick "is this tier structure sane" checks → inline.

## Red flags
| Thought | Reality |
|---|---|
| "I'll suggest a price point that sounds right." | You have no cost or willingness-to-pay data. Ask for both, or label the number a placeholder to test. |
| "Add a cheaper tier to catch price-sensitive buyers." | A low tier can cannibalise the middle and anchor everyone down. Model it against the existing base first. |
| "A 20% increase across the board is straightforward." | Not for the existing base. Separate new-logo pricing from migration, and name the churn assumption. |
| "Competitors charge $X, so match it." | Their cost base, segment, and packaging aren't yours. Price signals position — decide what yours should say. |

## Rules
- Never present a price recommendation as validated without willingness-to-pay evidence — pricing research, win/loss notes, or a live test.
- Model any change to an existing base separately from new customers, and state the assumption that drives the result.
- Flag anything needing finance or legal sign-off — revenue recognition, existing contract terms, regional pricing law — rather than working around it.
